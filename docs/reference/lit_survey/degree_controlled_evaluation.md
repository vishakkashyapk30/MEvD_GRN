# Degree-controlled evaluation of link predictors: literature survey

Compiled 2026-11-19. This survey looks for published precedents for our degree-controlled tests:

- **Degree-matched test (scMultiomeGRN):** each test link is paired with one of the 3 test non-links nearest to it in sorted (log(1+deg u), log(1+deg v)).
- **Caliper-matched test (scMultiomeGRN):** a link is kept only if some non-link has both endpoint log(1+deg) within 0.1 of the link's. One such non-link is drawn at random. Unmatched links are dropped.
- **BEAR-GRN target-in-degree-stratified AUROC:** in-degree 0 is its own stratum, then 20 quantile bins.
- **BEAR-GRN per-TF matching:** link (t,g) is paired with non-link (t,g'), where in-degree(g') = in-degree(g).

**How things were verified.** "Verified" means I read the primary text: full text through PMC, arXiv, bioRxiv PDF or publisher PDF, or at least the PubMed abstract where noted. Quotes are copied from those texts. Two exceptions are flagged in the table: the Li et al. 2025 (BMC Biol) quotes came through an automated page extractor, so re-check their wording before putting them in a paper. **UNVERIFIED** marks anything I could not confirm from a primary source. Preprints are labelled.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Degree-matched negatives for link-prediction evaluation are established in peer-reviewed work.**
   - Aiyappa et al., ICML 2025 (distributional matching).
   - Cappelletti et al., *Bioinformatics Advances* 2024 (degree-aware negatives, explicitly for the evaluation phase).
   - Cabrera-Andrade et al., *Frontiers in Bioinformatics* 2026 ("degree-matched" negatives preserving the **endpoint log-degree-bin** distribution, the closest in form to ours).
   - Yu et al., *Bioinformatics* 2010 (per-protein degree-balanced negatives).
   - Li et al., *BMC Biology* 2025 (negatives matched exact-then-nearest on endpoint degree sum, but for training only).
   - For GRNs specifically, Stock et al. (bioRxiv 2025/2026, **preprint**) do this for TF→target links.
2. **Degree-stratified evaluation is also published.**
   - Yılmaz et al., *PNAS* 2025: AUPR within 3×3 endpoint-degree classes.
   - Bellogín et al., *Inf. Retrieval J.* 2017: accuracy within item-popularity percentiles, then averaged. This is a direct analogue of the BEAR in-degree bins.
   - Yang, Lichtenwalter & Chawla, *KAIS* 2015: AUROC within geodesic-distance strata. Same logic, different stratifier.
3. **I found no paper, peer-reviewed or preprint, that applies a caliper (a tolerance on both endpoint degrees, dropping unmatched positives) to build link-prediction test negatives.** The caliper is standard matching methodology from observational statistics:
   - Cochran & Rubin 1973; Rosenbaum & Rubin 1985.
   - Austin 2011 for caliper width.
   - Stuart 2010 for the trade-off of dropping unmatched units.

   Evaluating a classifier against covariate-matched controls has a formal theory: the covariate-adjusted ROC of Janes & Pepe (2008, 2009) and Pepe, Fan & Seymour (2013). **The honest framing is:** "we apply caliper matching (Rosenbaum & Rubin 1985; Austin 2011) on endpoint log-degree to estimate degree-adjusted discrimination in the sense of Janes & Pepe (2009), extending the degree-matched negative sampling of Aiyappa et al. (2025) and Cabrera-Andrade et al. (2026) from distribution-level to pair-level matching."
4. **One exact precedent for BEAR per-TF in-degree matching exists, but it is a weak preprint.** Novák et al., arXiv 2610.32510 (Oct 2026, dark-web hyperlink graph) keep the source node and pick the absent target with the closest training in-degree. Stock et al. (preprint) do the same thing distributionally: same TF, target sampled in proportion to in-degree.

## 2. Ranked shortlist with suggested phrasing

### (a) Degree-matched test (nearest neighbour in endpoint log-degree)

1. **Aiyappa et al. 2025 (ICML)**. *Phrasing:* "Uniformly sampled non-links have lower endpoint degree than held-out links, so degree alone nearly solves the task (Aiyappa et al., 2025). Following their degree-corrected benchmark, we draw test non-links whose endpoint degrees match those of the test links. We match each pair (nearest neighbour in log-degree) rather than the overall distribution."
2. **Cabrera-Andrade et al. 2026 (Front. Bioinform.)**. *Phrasing:* "This mirrors the 'degree-matched' negative contract of BioGraphBench-PPI, which preserves the endpoint log-degree-bin distribution of positive test pairs." This is the closest published form: log-degree of both endpoints, used for test negatives.
3. **Cappelletti et al. 2024 (Bioinform. Adv.)**. *Phrasing:* "Degree-aware sampling of evaluation negatives has been shown to remove inflated performance in biomedical graph learning (Cappelletti et al., 2024)."
4. **Yu et al. 2010 (Bioinformatics)**, with **Park & Marcotte 2011** as the caveat. *Phrasing:* "Degree-balanced negatives were introduced for PPI prediction to remove hub-driven inflation (Yu et al., 2010). As Park & Marcotte (2011) note, such sets estimate discrimination beyond degree, not population-level accuracy, so we report them alongside the standard test, not instead of it."
5. **Stock et al. (preprint)**, for the GRN setting. *Phrasing:* "Degree-only rankers match or beat supervised GRN models under standard negative sampling, and degree-aware negatives remove this (Stock et al., preprint)." Label it as a preprint.
6. Optional mechanism citation: **Li et al. 2025 (BMC Biol)**, which matches exact-then-nearest on endpoint degree sum. Say it was used for **training** negatives.

### (b) Caliper-matched test, plus the general "matched evaluation" justification

1. **Janes & Pepe 2008 (Biometrics)** and **Pepe, Fan & Seymour 2013 (Acad Radiol)**. *Phrasing:* "We treat degree as a nuisance covariate and construct a matched case-control evaluation, in which controls (non-links) are matched to cases (links) on the covariate. Matching 'eliminates the potential for covariates to make the [test] appear more accurate than it is' (Pepe et al., 2013; Janes & Pepe, 2008)."
2. **Rosenbaum & Rubin 1985 (Am Stat)** and **Austin 2011 (Pharm Stat)**, plus **Cochran & Rubin 1973** if a classic source is wanted. *Phrasing:* "Matches are accepted only within a caliper of 0.1 on log(1+degree) for both endpoints (caliper matching; Rosenbaum & Rubin, 1985; Austin, 2011)."
3. **Stuart 2010 (Stat Sci)** with **Rosenbaum & Rubin 1985a**, for the dropped links. *Phrasing:* "As usual with caliper matching, links without an acceptable match are discarded. The resulting estimate refers to the region of common support (Stuart, 2010), and we report the fraction retained."

## 3. Explicit statement on the caliper

**No published or preprint work I found uses a caliper on node degree (a tolerance on both endpoint degrees, with unmatched positives dropped) to build link-prediction evaluation negatives.** I ran targeted searches combining "caliper", "degree-matched", "matched negatives", "link prediction", "propensity score" and "negative sampling", and read the closest candidates.

The closest equivalents, in order:
1. **Cabrera-Andrade et al. 2026**: test negatives preserve the endpoint **log-degree-bin** distribution. Coarse bins act like a tolerance, but matching is distributional and no positives are dropped.
2. **Li et al. 2025**: pair-level exact-then-neighbouring match on endpoint **degree sum**, with a widening tolerance, for training negatives only.
3. **Novák et al. 2026 (preprint)**: pair-level nearest target in-degree with the source fixed (directed graphs, test negatives).
4. **Aiyappa et al. 2025** and **Cappelletti et al. 2024**: distribution-level degree-proportional negatives.
5. **Guney et al. 2016**: degree-binned matching of random node sets, used for null distributions rather than test negatives.

The caliper itself is standard matching methodology in statistics: Cochran & Rubin 1973, Rosenbaum & Rubin 1985, Austin 2011, Stuart 2010. Its use to evaluate a classifier against matched controls is covered by Janes & Pepe 2008/2009 and Pepe et al. 2013. **The defensible claim is therefore methodological transfer, not precedent:** standard caliper matching from observational studies, applied to a covariate (degree) that recent link-prediction work has shown to confound evaluation.

## 4. Caveats a reviewer may raise (with sources)

- **Different estimand.** Matched or stratified AUROC measures discrimination *at fixed degree*, not deployment accuracy over all pairs. Report it next to the standard test (Park & Marcotte 2011; Janes & Pepe 2008).
- **Pooled AUROC on a matched set is conservative.** Pepe et al. 2013 show that ignoring the matching covariate when pooling "attenuates" the ROC toward the diagonal relative to the AROC, whenever the score varies with the covariate.
  - Our degree-matched and caliper AUROCs pool across degree levels, so they are a lower-biased estimate of degree-adjusted AUC.
  - A pair-level concordance (fraction of matched pairs where the link outscores its own matched non-link) or a stratum-weighted AUROC is the more direct estimator. Consider reporting one of them.
- **Dropping unmatched links changes the population.** The caliper estimate applies only to links that have a degree-comparable non-link, typically excluding the highest-degree hub–hub links (Stuart 2010; Rosenbaum & Rubin 1985a). Report the retained fraction and the degree profile of dropped links.
- **"0.5 by construction" holds only up to the caliper.** Within a 0.1 log-unit caliper, a degree-only rule can still exploit residual differences. It is ≈0.5, and exactly 0.5 only in expectation under random tie-breaking. A sensitivity sweep over caliper width (e.g. 0.05, 0.1, 0.2) is the usual robustness check; Austin 2011 frames width choice as a bias–variance trade-off.
- **Matching on degree removes degree's own contribution.** Janes & Pepe 2008 note that matching "greatly complicates estimation of the incremental value of the marker" over the matching covariate. So the degree-controlled tests cannot also tell you how much a model's degree-awareness helps in deployment.
